# Welcome to Watercolourlove

## The idea

Every painter who works from a photo faces the same two questions. Which colours are really in this picture, once you look past the thousand small variations a camera records? And how do I mix each of them from the paints in my box, with how much water?

Watercolourlove answers both. It identifies the colours that carry the picture, groups them into a manageable number of mixes and works out a recipe for every mix from your own paints: which paints, in what ratio, at which strength. Around that sit the tools for reading a reference the way a painter does: the colour areas of the picture, blocking of light and shadow, values, close-ups. The app knows that paint is transparent, that water makes a colour lighter, and that the white of the paper is the lightest thing you have. Printouts with a grid and numbered areas are there too, as aids when you want them, not as the point.

7. **Help** shows the names of all buttons at once.

## Who it is for

Watercolourlove is made for people who take watercolour seriously. Perhaps you have painted for years and want a faster, more reliable way from a reference photo to a plan. Perhaps you are learning and want to understand why a colour turns out the way it does, instead of guessing. Either way, the app does the measuring, and you keep the decisions.

It is not a filter that makes a photo look painted. Nothing here paints for you. The app proposes; you choose the colours, the shapes and the order, and the brush is in your hand.

## What it can and cannot do

The recipes are calculated, not measured on your paper. The app knows one reference colour for each paint and models how paints combine and how water thins them. That is a good estimate, often a very good one, but your paper, your water, your brush and the particular tube in front of you all have a say. Treat every recipe as a starting point and let a test strip on the same paper decide. The chapter [Calibrating your paints](calibrating.html) shows how to bring the reference colours closer to your own.

Two more limits worth knowing from the start:

- A paint is never used darker than its reference colour. Watercolour cannot get darker by adding more of the same paint beyond a certain point; it needs a second, darker paint. The recipes follow that rule.
- Very small, very dark details (an eye, a gap between stones) are easier to paint by eye at the end than to follow as areas. The app keeps them as separate small areas, but you decide how literally to take them.

## Two ways to paint

**Mix.** Every colour of the picture is mixed on the palette, and each area is painted once with its mix. One mix is used at up to five strengths of water, from a very watery wash to a concentrated one, so twenty mixes give you far more than twenty colours on paper. This is the method most of this manual describes, and the one to start with.

**Layer.** A few base paints are glazed over each other, light to dark, each as one prepared wash. Greens, oranges and darks appear where the layers overlap. It is the classic glazing technique, planned for you step by step. The chapter [Layer mode](layer-mode.html) explains it.

## What you need

- A photo. Clear light and a few large shapes work best; the chapter [Pictures](pictures.html) explains why.
- Your watercolours, and the knowledge of which ones you own. The [paint box](paint-box.html) knows the colours of Schmincke Horadam, Winsor & Newton Professional and Michael Harding, and takes any other paint as a colour of your own.
- Watercolour paper (300 g/m², ideally cotton), taped to a board, two or three brushes, a palette with wells, two jars of water and a pencil.
- A printer or a second screen beside your desk, for the plan.

## How this manual is organised

The manual has four parts.

- **Getting started.** This introduction, and [From photo to painting](quick-start.html): one picture from the first click to the last brush stroke, in fourteen steps. If you read only one chapter, read that one.
- **The app.** Every part of the app in depth: [pictures](pictures.html), the [paint box](paint-box.html), the [colour areas](colour-areas.html), [mixes and recipes](mixes.html), [layer mode](layer-mode.html), [values](values.html), [printing](printing.html) and your [account](account.html).
- **Painting with watercolour.** What the app assumes and what you do at the desk: [materials](materials.html), [how watercolour works](how-watercolour-works.html), [mixing](mixing.html), [washes](washes.html), [glazing](glazing.html), [values and simplifying](values-and-simplifying.html) and [calibrating your paints](calibrating.html). These chapters stand on their own, with or without the app.
- **Reference.** The [glossary](glossary.html) with every term in English and German, and [troubleshooting](troubleshooting.html).

Throughout the manual you will see one picture: the sample photo **Llyn Idwal**, a lake below the mountains of Snowdonia in Wales, analysed with the Schmincke Horadam paints of the stock and twenty mixes. The app finds 52 colours in those 20 mixes. You can open the same photo under **Samples** and follow along.

## Your pictures stay with you

Everything is worked out on your device. The photo is never uploaded, and your pictures, paint boxes and collection are kept in this browser on this device, not in your account.
