# Layer mode

## Glazing instead of mixing

In mix mode every colour is mixed on the palette and every area is painted once. Layer mode works the other way round, the way many watercolourists build a painting: a few transparent washes, each from a single paint, laid one over the other. A yellow over a blue gives green on the paper; a second coat of the same blue over the first deepens it; an earth over both quietens everything it touches.

Switch the method in the settings from **Mix** to **Layer**. The app then chooses for every area which layers it receives and how many coats of each, so that the finished glaze comes as close to the photo as possible for the work it costs. A coat that would add too little is left out.

![Layer mode with Llyn Idwal: four layers and one accent](shot:layers)

### When to choose layer mode

- **For luminous colour.** Colours mixed on the paper by glazing stay clearer than the same colours mixed in a well, because each layer is a single pigment.
- **For large, calm pictures.** Landscapes, skies and water suit it well: each wash covers large parts of the paper.
- **For learning.** You prepare four or five puddles instead of twenty, and you see exactly what each layer contributes.

Mix mode is the better choice for pictures with many distinct, small colours, for opaque or strongly granulating paints, and when you want each area finished in one pass. Chapter [Glazing](glazing.html) covers the painting technique itself.

## Base paints and settings

Layer mode paints with a few **base paints**, one layer each, at most six. They are kept apart from the paints selected for mixing: you can have a palette of twenty for mix mode and four base paints for layer mode in the same picture.

The app proposes base paints for the picture (**Auto**). To choose them yourself, open the paint box from the layer list with **Show paint box**. In layer mode its top part is called **Paint box: base paints**; click a chip to add or remove it. When a paint you chose adds too little to this picture, the list says so, for example "Not needed for this picture" or "No layer for ... it adds too little here".

In the settings, layer mode has its own controls:

| Setting | What it decides |
| --- | --- |
| **Layers** | How many base paints the app proposes, 2 to 6. With paints chosen by hand it shows how many you chose. |
| **Accents** | Up to three small, strong touches painted last: first for a colour the washes miss, then for the darks. |
| **Max. coats** | How often one wash may be painted over itself, 1 to 3. More coats give deeper tones from fewer paints, at the cost of more drying. |
| **Simplify areas** | Its own value in layer mode. Every layer has to be painted by hand, so calm shapes matter even more than in mix mode. |

## The layer list

The list shows each layer in painting order, from light to dark, and the accents below it under **Accents, painted last**.

Each row gives the paint, how to prepare its wash, and its coats. The wash is written as a ratio and a consistency, for example **1:9 with water, like coffee**: one part paint to nine parts water. The consistencies are the ones painters use:

| Consistency | Water to one part paint | Feel |
| --- | --- | --- |
| tea | 20 parts and more | a faint tint, barely coloured |
| coffee | about 8 to 15 parts | clearly coloured, still very fluid |
| milk | about 3 to 7 parts | a full wash |
| cream | 1 to 3 parts | strong, flows slowly |
| butter | hardly any water | paint as it comes from the tube |

The chip next to the paint shows one stroke of this wash on white paper. Test your puddle against it on a scrap of the same paper before you start.

> **Note:** The ratios assume that the swatch of a paint is about one part paint to one part water. Like every recipe in the app, they are estimates from one swatch. The stroke on your scrap decides.

### Ticking and showing a layer alone

The box in front of a layer switches it on and off in the picture. Untick the yellow and you see what the picture would be without it. The ticks only change what the picture shows; the paints themselves are changed in the paint box.

Click a layer's row and the picture shows that layer alone. The numbers on the picture are now **coats**: paint every numbered area once, let it dry, then paint the areas marked 2 again.

![One layer alone: the numbers are coats](shot:layer-solo)

**All** shows every layer again.

### What a spot receives

Tap a spot on the picture. A card shows **This spot**: the colour of the photo next to the layered colour, how close they are, and **Layers on this spot**, the coats of every layer it gets. Layers that leave it unpainted are named too. From the card you can jump to the step that paints a coat.

## Painting step by step

**Paint step by step** turns the plan into a sequence you can follow at the desk. Every step is one coat of one layer, in the order you paint them.

![Step 3: where to paint the wash](shot:layer-step)

Each step tells you what to mix, where to paint it and to let it dry completely before the next. Three views show the step:

- **Where to paint:** the paper after this step, with the areas painted now in full colour and the rest faded.
- **This wash:** only the areas of this step, filled with one stroke of the wash.
- **So far:** the paper with every layer up to and including this step. This is what your painting should look like now.

**Previous** and **Next** move through the steps. The last step tells you that you are done.

> **Tip:** Keep **So far** on screen while the paper dries. It shows the next stage before you commit to it, and it is the best moment to decide whether the next coat is really needed.

## Printing the layers

**Print / PDF** in layer mode gives the finished picture first, then one sheet per layer: its paint and wash, the areas to paint with their coat numbers, and short instructions such as "Coat 1 on every numbered area" and which numbers get a second coat. Accents get a sheet of their own, to be painted last when everything else is dry. See [Printing](printing.html).
