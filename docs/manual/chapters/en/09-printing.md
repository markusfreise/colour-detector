# Printing and exporting

## The printed sheet

**Print / PDF** in the column on the left prints the picture on a page of its own, followed by its legend: every mix with its paints, so that you can paint without the screen.

![The legend: every mix as a card with its washes and paints, four to a row](shot:print)

The sheet has two parts:

- **The picture**, on the first page, exactly as it is shown at the moment of printing: the view you chose, with or without outlines and numbers. For the desk, print it in **Mixed paint** with outlines and numbers on, and with the grid if you copy the drawing square by square (see below).
- **The legend**, from the second page, headed with the name of the picture. One card per mix, four to a row, so that about twenty mixes fit on a page. At the top of each card, always on the left, is the mix at full strength, its C wash, with its number and its share of the picture; next to it its other washes, darkest first, with their letters and shares. Below it is the recipe as a bar, then the paints, one under the other, with their parts, pan or tube and number. Mixes that are left as white paper say so.

Choose **Save as PDF** as the printer in the print dialog of your browser to keep the sheet as a file, for example on a tablet next to the painting.

> **Tip:** Tick off each mix on the printed legend once its areas are painted. It is the simplest way not to lose your place in a picture with twenty mixes.

### In layer mode

In layer mode the sheet is the finished picture followed by one page per layer: the paint and how to prepare its wash, the areas to paint with their coat numbers, and what to do ("Coat 1 on every numbered area", then which numbers get a second coat). The accents follow last. Chapter [Layer mode](layer-mode.html) explains the coats.

## The grid

**Grid** in the tools above the picture, left of **Outlines**, lays a grid of square cells over the picture. Each click steps on from no grid to white, grey and black lines, so that the grid stays visible on light and on dark pictures.

![The grid: eight columns of square cells over the picture](shot:view-grid)

**Grid columns** in the settings decides how many cells fit across the picture, from 2 to 30. The rows have the same height as the columns are wide, so every cell is square; the last row is cut off where the picture ends.

The grid is drawn in every view and goes onto the printed sheet and into the JPG. Draw the same number of columns on your paper, with cells as square as on the screen, and the picture is ready to copy cell by cell (see the next section).

## Transferring the drawing

If you would rather draw on your own paper, you have several options, from fastest to most personal:

- **Light box or window.** Tape the printed picture, outlines on, behind the watercolour paper against a light box or a sunny window and trace the outlines with a hard, light pencil (2H or H).
- **Graphite transfer.** Shade the back of the printout with a soft pencil (4B), lay it on the paper, and trace the lines with a ballpoint. Use as little pressure as you can; dents in the paper catch paint.
- **Grid by eye.** Turn on the grid, print the picture with **Mixed paint** and draw the same grid of light lines on the paper. Copy one square at a time. It is slower and trains your eye.

Write the labels (6L, 3R) small and faint inside the areas. A light wash will still let you read them; a dark wash covers them. You do not need every outline. Leave out what you would rather paint freely, and the painting will be yours, not a copy.

![Tracing the drawing at the window](photo:09-tracing)

## Paper size and scale

The browser's print dialog decides the paper. The picture has the first page to itself and is fitted to it. On an A4 or Letter page a landscape photo comes out at about 19 by 13 cm, a portrait photo at up to about 26 cm high. For a larger painting:

- choose a larger paper size in the print dialog if your printer has it,
- or save as PDF and print the picture at a copy shop,
- or use the grid method to scale it up by hand.

Keep the ratio of the photo. A painting cropped differently from the plan will need its own plan: crop the photo first, then open it in the app.

## Export as JPG

**Export as JPG** saves the picture as it is drawn on the screen: the view, the outlines, the numbers and, in layer mode, the layers that are shown. It has the size of the picture on the screen. Use it to send the plan to your tablet, to compare stages of a series, or to keep a record of your choices next to the photo.
