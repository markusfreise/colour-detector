# Printing and exporting

## The printed sheet

**Print / PDF** in the column on the left prints the picture followed by its legend: every mix with its paints, so that you can paint without the screen.

![The printed sheet: the picture, then every mix with its washes and paints](shot:print)

The sheet has two parts:

- **The picture**, exactly as it is shown at the moment of printing: the view you chose, with or without outlines and numbers. For the desk, print it in **Mixed paint** with outlines and numbers on, or in **Template to print** (see below).
- **The legend**, headed with the name of the picture. One row per mix: its number, its washes side by side with their letters and their share of the picture, the paints with their parts, pan or tube and number, and the recipe as a bar. Mixes that are left as white paper say so.

Choose **Save as PDF** as the printer in the print dialog of your browser to keep the sheet as a file, for example on a tablet next to the painting.

> **Tip:** Tick off each mix on the printed legend once its areas are painted. It is the simplest way not to lose your place in a picture with twenty mixes.

### In layer mode

In layer mode the sheet is the finished picture followed by one page per layer: the paint and how to prepare its wash, the areas to paint with their coat numbers, and what to do ("Coat 1 on every numbered area", then which numbers get a second coat). The accents follow last. Chapter [Layer mode](layer-mode.html) explains the coats.

## The template to print

**Template to print** is a view of the picture with the areas left empty: only their outlines and numbers, drawn in light blue. You find it among the views in the tools above the picture, on a phone in the menu.

![The template: outlines and numbers in light blue, nothing filled in](shot:view-template)

Light blue has a purpose. A printer makes it without black ink, so the lines stay faint and the paint covers them. You can use the template in two ways:

1. **Print it on watercolour paper and paint on it.** Many inkjet printers take thin watercolour paper (up to about 300 g/m² for some photo printers; check your printer's manual). Print the template, let the ink dry completely, tape the sheet down and paint the numbered areas directly.
2. **Print it on ordinary paper and transfer the drawing.** See the next section.

**Print / PDF** in this view prints the template and the legend: the sheet you paint from and the sheet you mix from.

> **Caution:** Inkjet ink can bleed when it gets wet. Test a corner of a printed template with a wet brush before you commit a good sheet of paper to it.

## Transferring the drawing

If you would rather draw on your own paper, you have several options, from fastest to most personal:

- **Light box or window.** Tape the printed template behind the watercolour paper against a light box or a sunny window and trace the outlines with a hard, light pencil (2H or H).
- **Graphite transfer.** Shade the back of the printout with a soft pencil (4B), lay it on the paper, and trace the lines with a ballpoint. Use as little pressure as you can; dents in the paper catch paint.
- **Grid by eye.** Print the picture with **Mixed paint** and draw a grid of light lines on both the print and the paper. Copy one square at a time. It is slower and trains your eye.

Write the labels (6L, 3R) small and faint inside the areas. A light wash will still let you read them; a dark wash covers them. You do not need every outline. Leave out what you would rather paint freely, and the painting will be yours, not a copy.

![Tracing the template at the window](photo:09-tracing)

## Paper size and scale

The browser's print dialog decides the paper. The picture is fitted to the width of the page and is at most 15 cm high, so that the legend starts on the same sheet. On an A4 or Letter page a landscape photo comes out at about 19 by 13 cm; a larger page adds little. For a larger painting:

- choose a larger paper size in the print dialog if your printer has it,
- or save as PDF and print the picture at a copy shop,
- or use the grid method to scale it up by hand.

Keep the ratio of the photo. A painting cropped differently from the plan will need its own plan: crop the photo first, then open it in the app.

## Export as JPG

**Export as JPG** saves the picture as it is drawn on the screen: the view, the outlines, the numbers and, in layer mode, the layers that are shown. It has the size of the picture on the screen. Use it to send the plan to your tablet, to compare stages of a series, or to keep a record of your choices next to the photo.
