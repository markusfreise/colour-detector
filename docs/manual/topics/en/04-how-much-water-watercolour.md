# How much water do I mix into watercolour paint?

In watercolour there is no white to lighten a colour. Water does it. The same two paints in the same ratio give a whisper of colour or a deep, rich passage, and the only difference is the water. That makes water the most important part of every mix, and the one painters most often leave to chance.

The trouble is that water is hard to see. A puddle looks darker in the well than it will on the paper, and every wash dries lighter than it looks wet. So "a bit more water" gives a different result every time. It helps to stop thinking of an endless scale and to work with a few fixed steps instead.

## Five levels, from tea to butter

Painters describe the strength of a wash by what it looks like in the well: tea, coffee, milk, cream, butter. Each word stands for a rough proportion of paint to water. "One part paint" means paint as it comes from the tube, or a pan worked up to the same creamy consistency.

| Level | Paint to water, roughly | In the well, like | What it is for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **W** very watery | 1 to more than 10 | tea | Tinted paper: skies near the horizon, mist, the light side of snow |
| **L** light | 1 to 4 up to 1 to 10 | coffee | Light washes over large areas, first layers |
| **M** medium | 1 to 2 up to 1 to 4 | milk | The body of most areas |
| **R** rich | about 1 to 1.5 | cream | Strong colour, shadows, foreground |
| **C** concentrated | about 1 to 1 | butter, it barely flows | The darkest darks, small accents painted last |

These proportions are estimates. Paints differ in strength: a phthalo at 1 to 10 is still strong, a yellow ochre at 1 to 4 already pale. Paper differs too. Treat the table as a starting point and let your own strip correct it.

## Paint one value strip

The most useful reference you can make takes ten minutes. Take one paint, a blue or an earth, and paint five patches of it on a strip of your painting paper, from tea to butter. Label each patch with its letter in pencil and let the strip dry completely.

Keep it beside you while you paint. When you mix a wash, test a stroke on a scrap and hold it next to the strip: you see at once whether you are at milk or still at coffee. After a few pictures the levels become a feeling in the brush.

## Mixing at the right level

1. **Mix strong first.** Put the paints into a clean well and stir them together with very little water. A ratio of two paints is far easier to judge in a small, strong puddle than in a big pale one.
2. **Add water for the level.** Now add clean water, a brush load at a time, until the puddle looks like the consistency you want.
3. **Mix enough.** A wash cannot be interrupted to mix more. Running out in the middle of an area leaves a hard edge.
4. **Thin for the lighter levels.** Take part of the strong puddle into a second well and add water for the lighter areas of the same colour. One puddle at several strengths keeps the colours of a picture related.
5. **Test it dry.** Paint a stroke on a strip of the same paper, let it dry and compare it in the same light.

When the test is too pale, add paint in the same proportion, not more of one paint. When it is too strong, add water. A wash that is too dark once it is on the paper can be lifted while wet with a squeezed brush; once dry, only a non-staining paint will lift.

## Why water and value go together

Water changes how light a colour is far more than it changes its hue. In a well-planned picture that is the point: the light side and the shadow side of a wall can be the same mix, once at coffee and once at cream. If you plan your values first and know which level each area needs, the choice of water stops being a guess. ## How Watercolourlove helps

Watercolourlove plans the water for you, colour by colour. When you open a reference photo, the app finds the colours that matter and groups them into mixes: one puddle each. Every area of the picture then carries a label made of the mix and its water level, from **W** to **C**. **6L** is mix 6 as a light wash, **6R** the same mix as a rich one. You mix 6 once and thin it for the L areas, and never have to match the same colour twice.

Click a mix in the colour list and only its areas stay on the picture, in all their washes, so you see where the puddle goes and at which strengths before you mix it.

The app never uses a paint stronger than its own swatch. Water only makes a paint lighter; a colour darker than one paint can be gets a second paint in the recipe, not less water. All of it is worked out on your device; the photo is never uploaded.

## What a water level cannot know

The proportions behind each level assume that a paint's reference colour is about one part paint to one part water. Your paints may be stronger or weaker, your brush may hold more or less, and your paper decides how light a wash dries. So the levels are a shared language between the app and your palette, not a measurement. The test strip on your paper decides, and when a paint keeps coming out too light or too dark, [calibrate it](calibrating.html) from your own swatch.

## Questions and answers

### What is the right ratio of paint to water in watercolour?

There is no single ratio; it depends on how light the area should dry. As a rough guide: 1 to more than 10 for a tint like tea, 1 to 4 up to 1 to 10 for a light wash, 1 to 2 up to 1 to 4 for a medium wash, about 1 to 1.5 for a rich one and about 1 to 1 for paint so concentrated it barely flows.

### What do tea, milk and cream mean in watercolour?

They describe how a wash looks and flows in the well. Tea is barely tinted water, coffee clearly coloured but very fluid, milk a full wash, cream strong and slow to flow, butter paint almost as it comes from the tube.

### Why does my watercolour dry so much lighter?

Wet paint looks darker because water fills the surface of the paper. As it evaporates, more light scatters back and the wash looks lighter and duller. Judge every wash dry, on a strip of the same paper.

### How do I make a watercolour darker?

Use less water, or add paint in the same proportion. But no paint gets darker than its own colour at full strength: for a darker colour, mix in a second, darker paint.

### How does Watercolourlove show the water level?
