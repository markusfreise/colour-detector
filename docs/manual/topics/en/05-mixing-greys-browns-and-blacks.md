# How do I mix greys, browns and blacks in watercolour without mud?

Look at a photo of a hillside and count the bright colours. There are usually few. The rest are greys that lean blue or green, browns that lean red or olive, and darks that are almost black but not quite. These quiet colours carry a picture, and they are the ones that go wrong most often: a grey that turns flat, a brown that turns heavy, a dark that dries chalky and dead.

The cause is nearly always the same. A neutral is a colour from which most of the light has been taken, and every pigment in the puddle takes its share. With two well-chosen paints, the same grey stays clear enough to glow.

## Complementary pairs: the heart of every neutral

Two paints that lie opposite each other on the colour wheel cancel each other out. Mixed in balance they give a grey; mixed with one in the lead they give a grey that leans towards it. That is the whole method, and it is more useful than any tube of grey.

| Pair | What it gives |
|---|---|
| Ultramarine and burnt sienna | Every grey from cool blue-grey to warm brown-grey; the classic for skies, rock and shadow |
| Ultramarine and a warm orange | Clean slate greys and deep, cool darks |
| A violet and yellow ochre | Soft, warm greys for distance |
| A green and a crimson | Dark greens, near-blacks and deep shadow under trees |

**Ultramarine and burnt sienna** deserve their reputation. With more blue the grey is cool, a winter sky or the shadow side of a wall; in balance it is a quiet neutral; with more sienna it turns brown, a slope, a tree trunk, wet earth. Concentrated, the pair gives a dark that most people take for black. Ultramarine granulates, so the grey keeps a little texture; for a smooth grey choose a non-granulating blue.

## Browns are dark oranges

A brown is an orange or a red with less light in it. You can mix it from an orange and a little blue, or start from an earth such as burnt sienna or burnt umber and shift it: a touch of red warms it, a touch of blue cools and darkens it. Earth pigments are the cleanest browns you can buy, because they are a single pigment.

## Why black from the tube looks dead

Black and Payne's grey are useful, and many painters keep one. On its own, though, a black from the tube is a dark without colour, and a shadow in nature always has colour in it: the shadow on grass is green, on stone it is the colour of the stone, under a blue sky it leans blue. Black also greys every colour it touches. A green with black in it reads as dirty in a way that a green darkened with its complement does not.

Use black as one paint of a pair, if at all: a little of it to deepen a mix that is already the right hue.

## Darks need dark paints

There is a limit that many mixes run into. A paint cannot get darker than its own colour at full strength. More water makes it lighter, more pigment brings it towards its masstone and then stops. A yellow, however much you load the brush, never reaches a deep olive on its own.

So a dark is not "more of the same paint", it is a darker paint in the mix. When the shadow under a tree is darker than your green can go, add a blue or a crimson, not more green. And build darks from **transparent** paints: opaque pigments scatter light and make a dark chalky, which is the most common reason a deep shadow dries grey.

## Keeping neutrals clean

- **Two paints, three at most.** Check the pigments on the labels. Two paints with three pigments each are already six in the puddle.
- **Mix in the palette, not on the paper.** Neutrals scrubbed together on the sheet lift the layer below and turn to mud.
- **Test it dry.** Greys dry lighter and cooler than they look wet. Judge them on a strip of the same paper.

## Questions and answers

### How do I mix grey in watercolour without black?

Mix two complementary paints, such as ultramarine and burnt sienna. In balance they give a neutral grey; more blue makes it cool, more sienna makes it warm. Water sets how light the grey dries.

### Why do my watercolour greys turn muddy?

Usually because there are too many pigments in the puddle, an opaque paint in the mix, or the wash was worked on the paper while it dried. Use two transparent paints, mix in the palette and lay the wash once.

### How do I mix a watercolour black?

Mix a dark, transparent pair concentrated: ultramarine with burnt sienna, or a phthalo green with a crimson. Such a black has colour in it and stays alive, unlike most blacks from the tube.

### Why can't I get my mix dark enough?

A paint cannot get darker than its own colour at full strength. To go darker, add a second, darker paint to the mix instead of more of the first.
