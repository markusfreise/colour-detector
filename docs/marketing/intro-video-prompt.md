# Prompt: Watercolourlove introduction video

For an animation tool such as Claude Motion. Attach the reference photo (landscape, clear light and
shadow, a few large shapes) and the logo. Everything in the film is derived from that one photo.

---

Create a calm, premium introduction animation for **Watercolourlove**, a web app for watercolour
painters. Length 40 seconds, 16:9, 1920 x 1080, 30 fps. Also deliver a 15-second cut in 9:16
(1080 x 1920) for social media from scenes 1, 2, 3 and 8.

**What the app does (the story to tell):** it takes a photo, finds its main colours, tells you how
to mix each one from the watercolours you own, and turns the photo into flat, numbered areas: a
paint-by-numbers plan for a real watercolour. The film shows that transformation, from photo to plan
to painting, using the attached reference photo.

**Look and feel**
- Quiet, considered, made by a painter. No tech-startup look: no gradients, no glow, no glass
  effects, no particles, no 3D, no fast cuts, no emoji, no icons flying in.
- Background: warm paper white `#f7f4ee`. Text and lines: near-black `#242424`. The only saturated
  colours on screen are the photo and the paint.
- Type: a clean sans serif, bold for headlines, set large with plenty of space. Short lines, at most
  six words on screen at once.
- Motion: slow, eased, organic. Transitions behave like water and pigment: colour spreads, settles
  and dries; edges soften before they sharpen. Each scene holds long enough to read.
- Real watercolour texture where paint appears: cold-pressed paper grain, granulation, soft blooms,
  pigment pooling at the edges of a wash.
- Sound (suggestion): a sparse piano or guitar, the sound of a brush in water, paper being taped.

**Scenes**

1. **The photo (0 to 4 s).** The reference photo fills the frame and drifts slowly. Text, bottom
   left: "Every painting starts with a photo."
2. **The colours are found (4 to 10 s).** The photo calms down: detail melts into flat areas of
   colour, region by region, as if the colour settles out of water. Thin dark outlines draw
   themselves around the areas. Text: "Watercolourlove finds the colours that matter."
3. **The numbers (10 to 15 s).** Small labels appear inside the areas, one after another, like
   pencil notes: a mix number and a letter for the amount of water, for example `3M`, `6L`, `6R`,
   `11C`. Text: "Every area gets a number."
4. **A recipe (15 to 21 s).** One area brightens; the rest of the picture fades to paper white. A
   small white card slides up beside it showing the target colour and the mix, and a recipe line:
   `1.5 x 645 Caput mortuum + 1 x 515 Olive green · medium · excellent`. Text: "Mixed from the
   paints you own."
5. **One puddle, many washes (21 to 26 s).** A single puddle of that mix on a white porcelain
   palette. A brush draws five stripes from it on paper, from very watery to concentrated, each
   labelled W, L, M, R, C as it dries. Text: "One mix. Five washes."
6. **The plan on paper (26 to 33 s).** Top-down view of a desk: watercolour paper taped to a
   board, the printed plan beside it, a paint box, two jars of water. The pencil outlines appear on
   the paper; washes flow into the areas light to dark, in time-lapse, until the painting from the
   reference photo stands finished. Keep it painterly, not photo-real: the result must look like a
   watercolour, with white paper left in the lights.
7. **Side by side (33 to 36 s).** The finished watercolour next to the original photo, both small,
   on paper white. Text: "From photo to painting."
8. **End card (36 to 40 s).** The Watercolourlove logo centred on paper white. Below it:
   "A colour mix finder for artists" and `watercolourlove.de`. A last drop of colour spreads softly
   behind the logo and stops.

**Rules for text**
- English. Use exactly the lines above; no other words on screen except the labels and the recipe.
- Labels like `6L` in a small bold sans, the recipe in the same face as the card text.
- Never show a phone or a computer screen with a user interface; the app is suggested by the card,
  the labels and the outlines, not by screenshots.

**Deliver** the 40-second 16:9 film, the 15-second 9:16 cut, and a still of the end card.
