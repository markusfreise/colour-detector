Manual · Watercolourlove (web) and Tintmap (iPhone, iPad, Mac)

From a photo to a watercolour, one mix at a time

The app finds the main colours in a photo, tells you how to mix each one from the paints you own, and turns the photo into a map of flat, numbered areas. This manual walks through one picture from the first tap to the last brush stroke.

How to use Layer mode More options Reference Storyboard and shot list

About the pictures in this manual

Pictures marked App are real screens of the current version with the sample photo. Pictures marked Storyboard are generated sketches that stand in for the photos and video still to be shot. They propose angle and content only; the numbers match the shot list at the end.

You need

A photo, your watercolours, watercolour paper (300 g/m²) taped to a board, two or three brushes, a palette with wells, two jars of water, a pencil and a printer or a second screen.

Two methods

Mix: every colour is mixed on the palette and each area is painted once. Layer: a few thin washes are glazed over each other. The step-by-step below uses Mix.

Private

Everything is worked out on your device. The photo is not uploaded and there is no account. Only the optional AI help in the web app sends a small copy of the picture, and only after you agree.

How to use: step by step

Steps 1 to 7 happen in the app and take about ten minutes. Steps 8 to 14 happen at the desk.

Part 1 · Plan the picture in the app

1

Choose a photo

Press Choose photo and pick a picture, or Take photo on a phone or tablet. In Tintmap the button is New Image. Samples opens the built-in examples if you want to try the app first.

Photos with clear light and shadow and a few large shapes work best. The app starts analysing at once; set Show to Photo at any time to see the original again.

2

Tell the app which paints you own

Open Show paint box (Tintmap: Paint box). Every paint is a chip in its own colour with its number; P and T stand for pan and tube. The paints in the box at the top are the ones the recipes may use. Click a chip to move it in or out.

  • Auto lets the app pick the small set of paints that suits this picture. It is on for a new paint box and switches off when you select by hand.
  • Your own paints adds paints of any brand with their own colour.
  • New box keeps a separate selection, for example studio and travel.

Press Update mixes after a change.

3

Decide how detailed the picture should be

Three sliders set the amount of work:

  • Colours (2 to 32) is the number of mixes on your palette. Start with 12 to 20.
  • Simplify areas (0 to 12) merges small shapes into bigger, calmer ones. At 0 the map follows nearly every speck of the photo; around 7 it becomes paintable. With Pro it goes down to -2, where the areas follow the photo even more closely.
  • Max. paints limits a mix to one, two or three paints. Fewer paints give cleaner mixes.

Saturation and Contrast adjust the photo before it is analysed, and Variety decides whether small areas of another hue (lips, a red roof) keep a mix of their own.

4

Read the map

Every area carries a label such as 3M. The number is the mix, the letter is how much water goes into it: W very watery, L light, M medium, R rich, C concentrated. So 3L and 3C are the same puddle of paint, once thinned and once strong. That is why 20 mixes give many more colours on the paper: 67 in this sample ("67 in 20 mixes" at the top of the list).

The list Colours shows one block per mix: its base colour, a bar with the paints in the proportions of the recipe, and a bar with its washes.

5

Look up a mix

Click an area in the picture or a mix in the list. The rest of the picture goes blank, so you see exactly where this mix belongs. The card shows the Target colour next to Your mix and up to five recipes.

A recipe reads like this: 15.5 x P 230 plus 1 x P 662 means 15.5 parts of pan 230 to 1 part of pan 662. Medium is the amount of water, and 0.1 · excellent says how close the mix gets to the target. Click another recipe if you prefer its paints.

6

Change what you do not like

You are the painter, the photo is only a suggestion. With a colour selected:

  • Change colour picks a new target, warmer, cooler or entirely different. The recipes follow.
  • Edit only the clicked area detaches the clicked area from the others that share its colour.
  • Reset to photo returns to the colour of the photo.

Press Save to keep the picture with its settings and changes in the collection (Tintmap: Library).

7

Print the sheet

Export saves the picture as a JPG exactly as it is shown, in the view you have chosen.

Print / PDF gives the picture followed by every mix with its paints as bars. Print it for the desk, or choose "Save as PDF" in the print dialog and keep it on a tablet. The picture on the sheet carries the outlines and labels you need for transferring the drawing in step 9.

Part 2 · Paint it

8

Lay out the desk

Tape the paper to a board on all four sides. Put the printed sheet where you can see it without reaching over wet paint, the paint box and palette on the side of your painting hand, and two jars of water behind them: one for rinsing, one that stays clean.

9

Transfer the drawing

Copy the outlines onto the watercolour paper with a light pencil: trace them against a window or a light pad, or draw them by eye with the printed sheet beside you. Write the labels (3M, 6L) small inside the areas; a thin wash will still let you read them, so keep them faint.

You do not need every outline. Leave out what you would rather paint freely.

10

Mix the first colour and test it

Take the mix with the lightest washes first. Count the parts in brush loads: for 3 x 672 and 1 x 525, three loads of the first paint and one of the second into the same well. Then add water for the wash you need: a lot for W and L, little for R and C.

Paint a stroke on a scrap of the same paper, let it dry and hold it next to the chip on the sheet. Watercolour dries lighter than it looks wet. Too pale: more paint. Too strong: more water.

The recipes are estimates from one reference colour per paint. Trust the test strip more than the numbers.

11

Paint the light areas first

Fill every area of this mix and wash, then thin or strengthen the same puddle for its other letters. Select the mix in the app to see only its areas. Work from the lightest washes to the darkest, and from large areas to small ones.

Do not paint an area next to one that is still wet, unless you want the two to run into each other.

12

Let it dry

Wait until the paper is flat and no longer cool to the back of your hand. Use the pause to mix the next colour and tick off the finished mixes on the sheet.

13

Work through the mixes down to the darks

Repeat steps 10 to 12 for every mix. The picture comes together with the middle tones; the darkest, most concentrated washes go on last with a small brush. They are the smallest areas and do the most for the likeness: eyes, mouths, the shadows under hair.

14

Compare, correct, take off the tape

Put the painting next to the screen with Show on Mixed paint. Where an area is too pale, glaze it once more with the same mix. When everything is dry, pull the tape off flat and away from the picture.

Layer mode: glazing instead of mixing

Set Method to Layer. Instead of twenty mixes you prepare a few thin washes, one per base paint, and paint them over each other from light to dark. Greens, oranges and darks appear where the layers add up.

1

Check the layers

The list shows each layer with its paint, how to prepare the wash (for example 1:40 with water, like tea) and how many coats it gets. Accents are small strong touches painted last. Tick layers on and off to see what each one does, or click a layer to see it alone; the numbers in the picture are then coats.

Layers, Max. coats and Accents set the amount of work. The base paints are proposed by the app or chosen in the paint box.

2

Paint step by step

Press Paint step by step. Each step names the wash to mix, shows where to paint it and tells you to let it dry. Switch between Where to paint, This wash and So far; Next moves on. The chip shows what one stroke of the wash looks like on white paper: test yours against it on a scrap.

Print / PDF gives the finished picture and one sheet per layer.

For photos and video, shots 11 to 15 of the storyboard serve both methods: in layer mode each wash covers large parts of the paper, which films well from above.

More options

Three things you can leave alone at first.

A

Geometric areas

Set Areas to Geometric and every area becomes a polygon with a few straight edges, laid over each other from large to small. It works in both methods and is quick to draw with a ruler. Simplify areas makes the edges longer and the shapes fewer.

B

Views

Mixed paint shows what your paints can reach, Photo colour what the photo asks for, Photo the original. Template to print leaves the areas empty and draws their outlines and numbers in light grey: print it and paint on the sheet. Tintmap adds Overlay: the areas at half strength over the photo. Outlines and Numbers can be switched off for a clean look at the colours.

C

AI help (web app, optional)

With your own API key, an AI model looks at a small copy of the picture and chooses the colours to mix and the paints for them; the app still works out the areas and recipes. It asks only when you press the button and shows what the call costs. Without a key nothing is sent anywhere.

Tintmap: free and paid

Free with one saved picture and one paint box. A single purchase lifts both limits. No subscription.

Where the data is

Web: in the browser on this device. Tintmap: on the device. Nothing is synchronised.

If a colour looks wrong

The stock holds one reference colour per paint. Your own swatch decides; change the target or pick another recipe.

Notes for the shoot

Screens

Record the app as a screen recording and film the device only for context (02 to 05). Filmed screens flicker and the text is too small.

One fixed top-down camera

Shots 07, 08, 11 and 14 share one position. Mark the board on the desk with tape so it does not move between sessions.

Light

Window from the side opposite the painting hand, a white card as fill. No lamp mixed in, or the paint colours shift.

Which picture

The frames show a group portrait as a stand-in. Choose the motif first, analyse it, and print the sheet before the shoot so the labels in the film are the real ones.

Length

As drawn: about 90 seconds for a short film (5 s per frame), or 4 to 5 minutes as a tutorial with screen recordings for steps 1 to 7.

Stills

Landscape 16:9 for the manual. Shoot 01, 08 and 18 also upright for the App Store and social formats.
