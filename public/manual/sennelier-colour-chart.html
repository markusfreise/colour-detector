Which Sennelier watercolours do I need, and how do I read the chart?

Sennelier L'Aquarelle is a French artists' watercolour made with honey. Its colour chart lists 98 colours with their pigments and transparency. Here is how to read it, how to choose a small palette from it, and the whole range as Watercolourlove knows it.

Sennelier L'Aquarelle is known for its honey: it keeps the paint moist in the pan and makes it rewet easily, with bright, flowing colour. The range is large, and the chart tells you more than the name of each colour. Read it well and you can put together a small palette that mixes cleanly before you buy a single tube.

Watercolourlove is not affiliated with Sennelier. The swatches, pigments and transparency on this page are what the app uses, read from Sennelier's own published chart and list; those and the paint on your desk have the last word.

#The range

The chart in the app has 98 colours, taken from Sennelier's colour chart of 2012, in its order: whites, yellows, oranges, reds, rose and violets, blues, greens, earths, greys and blacks. Each has a three-digit number, as on the tube and the half pan. Sennelier counted 110 colours in 2022; the 12 added since 2012 are on no public chart of theirs, so they are not in the app yet.

For every colour, the chart gives its pigments and a transparency symbol. Of the 98, 47 are marked transparent, 23 semi-opaque and 28 opaque, and 56 are made of a single pigment. Sennelier also publishes a list of its granulating colours, 36 of them in 2022.

#How to read a maker's chart

Every maker prints the same handful of facts for each colour, each in its own symbols, explained in the key of the chart.

  • Number and name. The number identifies the paint. Sennelier's names exist in French and English; the number is the same in both.
  • Pigments. The Colour Index names, such as PB29 for ultramarine or PY153 for a warm transparent yellow. One pigment mixes more cleanly than a blend of three; a "hue" in the name means a colour imitated with other pigments.
  • Transparency. Transparent paints glaze cleanly; semi-opaque and opaque ones lay a veil over what lies below.
  • Staining. Whether the paint lifts once dry. Staining paints make good first layers.
  • Granulation. Whether the pigment settles into the grain of the paper and leaves a texture.
  • Lightfastness. How well the colour holds in daylight. For work you want to keep, choose the highest ratings.

#Choosing a small palette

The table below makes it easy to pick by pigment and transparency. A transparent palette of six could be:

  • 501 Lemon Yellow (PY3) and 578 Sennelier Yellow Light (PY153), a cool and a warm yellow,
  • 315 Ultramarine Deep (PB29) and 326 Phthalocyanine Blue (PB15:3), a warm and a cool blue,
  • 690 Rose Madder Lake (PV19), a cool red,
  • 211 Burnt Sienna (PBr7), for oranges, browns and greys with the ultramarine.

All six are marked transparent and single-pigment, so their mixes stay clear and they glaze well. Add 252 Yellow Ochre (PY43) when you want an opaque, earthy yellow for stone and sand.

Watch for duplicates: several colours share a pigment, such as the three ultramarines (PB29) or 635 Carmine and 690 Rose Madder Lake (both PV19). And some convenience colours are blends you can mix yourself: 819 Sap Green is PB29 with PY153, the ultramarine and the warm yellow above.

The chapter Paint box and palette goes into how many paints make a good palette, and Glazing in layers into why transparent paints matter for glazes.

#How the colours in the app were read

Each swatch is the median colour of a band 20 to 40 per cent down the swatch on Sennelier's chart, near its strong end; the app treats it as the paint at strength 1, the darkest it is used in a recipe. One colour, 917 Dioxazine Purple, changes hue so much from strong to pale on the chart that no single value fits it. It has no reference colour: its number fills in the name, and you set the colour yourself.

The colours on your screen are approximations of a printed swatch, and batches of paint differ. If a paint keeps mixing differently from what the app expects, calibrate it from a swatch of your own, and the recipes follow your paint.

#How Watercolourlove helps

With Pro, the Sennelier L'Aquarelle chart is part of the library of the paint box, together with the charts of three other makers. You search by name, number or pigment ("PB29" finds all three ultramarines) and click a colour to add it to your box. When you enter a paint by hand with the series "Sennelier L'Aquarelle", its number fills in name and colour.

With a picture you unlock, you mix from your own paints too: the 40 common colours of the library, or any paint you enter yourself.

#Questions and answers

#How many colours does Sennelier L'Aquarelle have?

Sennelier counted 110 colours in 2022. The app has the 98 on Sennelier's public chart of 2012; the 12 added since are on no public chart.

#Does Sennelier watercolour contain honey?

Yes. Sennelier makes L'Aquarelle with honey, which keeps the paint moist and lets it rewet easily in the pan.

#Which Sennelier colours are transparent?

On the chart, 47 of the 98 colours are marked transparent. The table on this page lists the transparency of each colour, so you can sort out the opaque ones before you buy.

#Can Watercolourlove mix from my Sennelier paints?

Yes. With Pro you add them from the chart by name, number or pigment. With a picture you unlock, you can enter them yourself, or use the common colours of the library.
