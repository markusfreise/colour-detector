What is a value study in watercolour, and how do I make one?

A value study is a small painting in light and dark only, done before the real one. It takes twenty minutes and decides more about the finished picture than any choice of colour. Here is what it is, how to make one, and how to read the values of a reference photo.

Value is how light or dark a colour is, whatever its hue. A pale yellow and a pale blue can share a value; so can a deep red and a mid green. A value study is the picture reduced to that one quality: a small sketch in light, middle and dark, painted or drawn before you start the colour version.

Painters say that value does the work and colour gets the credit. A painting with the right lights and darks reads as true even in odd colours. A painting with lovely colours and muddled values falls apart. A black and white photo of a good watercolour still works; a badly valued one turns to an even grey.

In watercolour this matters more than anywhere, because you cannot paint light back. The white of the paper is the only white you have. If you do not know before the first wash where the lightest lights are, you will lose them.

#Why value first, then colour

Your eye sorts a scene by colour; the picture is held together by value. When you paint from a photo, the bright colours catch the attention, and the large shapes of light and dark go unnoticed until the painting looks flat. A value study turns that order round. You decide the structure first, then colour only has to sit on something that already works.

It also makes colour easier. Once you know an area is "middle", you know how much water its mix needs, and you can be free with the hue.

#Two values: the notan

A notan, the Japanese word for the balance of light and dark, is the most radical study: the whole picture in two values, black and white. Take a marker and a sheet no larger than a postcard. Decide for every part of the picture whether it is light or dark, and fill in the dark. No middle, no detail.

Ten minutes with a notan shows whether the composition has a clear structure. If the light shapes are scattered into many small pieces, the painting will look busy; if one large light shape meets one large dark, it will read from across the room.

#Three values, then five

Most strong paintings can be described in three values: light, middle and dark. Before you paint, decide for each large shape of the picture which of the three it belongs to. Then split each into two where you need it, and you have five: the white of the paper, light, middle, dark and the darkest accents.

That is about as many as a watercolour needs. In practice the five values are five strengths of wash, from a tint of water to paint that barely flows.

#Making a thumbnail

  1. Keep it small. A thumbnail of about 10 by 7 cm stops you from drawing detail.
  2. Draw the large shapes with a few lines: horizon, the big masses, the main subject.
  3. Squint at the reference. Half close your eyes until the detail and the colour blur away, and what is left is the value pattern.
  4. Paint the values in one dark paint, a neutral tint, sepia or indigo, from the lightest wash to the darkest. Leave the paper white where the photo is lightest.
  5. Compare. Put the dry study next to the photo and squint at both. When they blur into the same shapes, the study has done its job.

#Reading values in a photo

A photo does not see the way you do. It picks one exposure for the whole scene, so the shadows turn too dark and too grey and a bright sky burns out to white. Do not copy the darkest darks literally: paint them a little lighter and give them colour, green in a shadow on grass, the colour of the stone in a shadow on rock.

#How Watercolourlove helps

Watercolourlove starts with the colours of your photo: it finds the colours that matter and works out how to mix each from your paints. For values it adds two tools and a view.

  • Blocking shows the photo as two or three flat tones, paper white and a strong dark, with a middle tone if you want one, in large masses numbered from the lightest. It is the notan done for you, and it marks the paper white you have to save. In the colour list, a mix that comes out lighter or darker than the tone of its area says so in red.
  • Monochrome turns the photo into a value study in up to 24 values and paints every value with the paints you give it, whatever their hue, with finer water steps than in colour.
  • Photo colour shows each area in the average colour of the photo there, the picture reduced to its shapes, which is the best view for a squint test on screen.

Analysis, recipes and the Photo colour view are part of the free version. Blocking comes with a picture you unlock or with Pro; Monochrome is part of Pro. The samples have everything open, so you can try both on them. The photo is never uploaded; everything is worked out on your device.

#What the app cannot decide for you

Blocking and Monochrome read the values the photo has, including the shadows the camera made too dark. Which light to keep, which dark to soften and what to leave out is your decision. Use the app to see the pattern, then make the study with your own brush; that is where you learn to see it without a screen.

#Questions and answers

#What is a value study in watercolour?

A small sketch of the picture in light and dark only, usually in one dark paint and three to five values, made before the painting. It settles where the lights and darks go before any colour is mixed.

#What is a notan?

A study in two values, black and white. It shows whether a composition has a clear structure of light and dark shapes, and it takes about ten minutes with a marker.

#How many values does a watercolour need?

Three carry most paintings: light, middle and dark. Split into five, with the white of the paper and the darkest accents, they are about as many as a watercolour can use.

#Which paint should I use for a value study?

A single dark, transparent paint such as a neutral tint, sepia or indigo. It goes from the palest wash to a deep dark without changing hue.

#Can Watercolourlove show me the values of my photo?
